TOO MUCH IS NEVER ENOUGH
A Spoiled Princess Story

Target runtime: ~92 seconds
Format: 9:16 vertical
Tone: whimsical fairy tale with modern, deadpan humor
Narrator: male, dry British voice, deadpan and slightly sarcastic
Music: light pizzicato comedy with playful strings, celesta accents and occasional woodwind punctuation

VISUAL LOOK

Photorealistic live-action fairy-tale palace. Elegant rather than overly ornate. Pale silver and ivory interiors, restrained crystal details, tall arched windows, polished pale marble floors, sculptural silver furniture and ruby-red accents. Whimsical proportions and objects, but generous negative space so characters and comedy remain visually clear. Soft candlelight mixed with cool window light. Classic fairy-tale interiors and clothing; modernity comes only through behavior and dialogue.

CAST

PRINCESS ZLATA

Princess Zlata, a pale young Slavic princess of about twenty with very long thick curly blonde hair, long lashes, pouty naturally red lips and a small beauty mark. She wears a tall crystal kokoshnik set with ruby, emerald and diamond stones, enormous black velvet puff sleeves and a sophisticated layered white embroidered gown with ruby-red accents, full structured skirts and luxurious fairy-tale detailing.

She is beautiful, whimsical, spectacularly spoiled, chronically bored and almost impossible to impress.

Her resting expression is a pout.

Voice: young woman, low and slightly bored, modern and dry, capable of sudden dramatic outbursts.

THE TSARINA

Zlata's mother, about forty-five, visibly an older version of her daughter. Curly blonde hair beginning to gray, fine wrinkles, tired eyes and an elegant face that looks increasingly exhausted as the film progresses.

She wears an oversized queen's crown and an elegant black-and-white royal gown with restrained ruby-red accents.

She remains regal even when frazzled.

Personality: anxious, indulgent, loving, exhausted and beginning to regret having given Zlata everything she ever wanted.

Recurring line:
“Please, darling.”

THE WITCH

A very old woman with the unsettling presence of a Soviet Baba Yaga.

She wears old-fashioned Soviet-grandmother clothing transformed subtly into fairy-tale costume: a dark headscarf, layered muted dress, heavy stockings, sturdy shoes, strange old rings and small eerie talismans sewn into her clothes.

She carries a crooked wooden cane.

Her entrance is genuinely ominous.

Her personality is not.

Voice: low, raspy, conversational, dry and completely unimpressed by royalty.

PRINCE NARSISSUS

Twenty-five, tall, blond, blue-eyed and almost offensively handsome.

He wears an elegant fitted velvet court suit with embroidered details and tall polished boots.

He behaves as though his arrival is the greatest gift any kingdom could receive.

Voice: smooth, confident and deeply pleased with himself.

PALACE MAIDS

Several palace maids in identical severe Russian fairy-tale uniforms in dusty blue and ivory.

Simple dresses, structured collars, ivory aprons and matching head coverings.

Their subdued appearance contrasts with Zlata and the ridiculous objects they continually carry into the throne room.

They remain almost completely deadpan.

SETS

THRONE ROOM

An airy fairy-tale throne room in pale silver and ivory. Tall arched windows, restrained crystal chandeliers, polished pale marble floor, elegant silver throne at the center, sparse silver candelabra and ruby-red velvet accents.

The room feels expensive but never cluttered.

PALACE GALLERY

Long symmetrical palace gallery with silver-and-ivory walls, tall windows, polished marble floors and occasional ruby-red curtains.

DARK DOORWAY

At the far end of the throne room is a tall shadowed doorway leading into near-blackness.

This is where the witch appears.

MUSIC

Light, mischievous orchestral comedy.

Pizzicato strings lead the score with small celesta flourishes and restrained woodwinds.

The music should never become cartoony.

Each rejected gift gets a tiny musical punctuation.

The witch entrance briefly drops into sparse, ominous low strings before returning immediately to dry pizzicato after her first line.

Prince Narsissus receives an absurdly romantic miniature fanfare.

The cupcake impact gets a brief musical stop.

Silence for the stunned beat.

Then the score bursts back in as the room celebrates.

IMPORTANT PERFORMANCE NOTES

Zlata should not behave like a screaming caricature throughout. Her comedy comes from the contrast between enormous royal spectacle and tiny modern reactions.

“Cute. Next!” is almost emotionless.

“My feet deserve better” is sincere.

“I'm prettier” is simply a factual observation in her mind.

“Is that gluten-free?” is asked as though the gigantic cupcake's existence is completely normal.

“Take him back” is immediate and decisive.

The Tsarina should remain elegant even at her most exhausted. Her increasing loss of composure should happen gradually.

The witch's entrance should visually promise a terrifying supernatural revelation. Her actual dialogue should puncture that expectation immediately.

Prince Narsissus should be genuinely handsome enough that Zlata's brief interest makes sense. His mirror kiss is not broad or silly; he performs it with complete sincerity.

For the final laugh, hold Zlata's resistance long enough for the audience to anticipate it. The cut back to the furious frosting-covered Narsissus is what finally breaks her.

The final wide should feel like a fairy-tale happy ending to an objectively ridiculous event.
